Dashboards for decisions

Operational dashboards earn their place when each metric helps someone choose the next action.

An operator opens the screen with a decision in mind. A vehicle needs attention, a charger may be down, a test needs review, inventory looks wrong, or a scientific lead needs enough evidence to move a hypothesis forward.

I start there. I give the operator the state, the owner, the last trusted update, and the next action. Extra charts can wait.

Compress the context

A strong operational view answers a few plain questions: what changed, who owns it, what needs attention, and how much confidence the team should place in the reading.

State, thresholds, provenance, exceptions, and empty states matter because field data arrives late, partial, duplicated, or noisy. Operators trust the screen when it handles those cases without making them guess.

Design for the operator

Real-world systems put users under time pressure. They need to know where attention should go. I reduce visual ambiguity and give the operator a clear path through the work.

I ask one question while designing these screens: after the team reads this, what can they do with more confidence?
